On discounting

A typical market comparison implies a discount ladder — roughly 10% off a $20 job, 17% off $3,000, and closer to 33% off $60,000. I am not using that ladder.

Discounting scales with job size here because cost does not. A $60,000 project still has one specification, one validation pass, and one delivery. Discounting it by 17% to win the contract means the margin collapses while the work stays identical, and the quality gets cut to cover the gap. You would pay less and get less, which is not a discount.

Where I will move on price: scope, not margin. A smaller deliverable costs less because it is genuinely less work. If the market rate is far below what the requirement needs, I will say the requirement is underpriced for anyone, rather than quietly deliver a thinner version of it.
